SkyBreaker: Supersonic Business Jet — Project Details

Overview

The SkyBreaker project focused on the conceptual and preliminary design of a Trans-Pacific Supersonic Business Jet (SSBJ) capable of nonstop flight from Los Angeles (LAX) to Tokyo (NRT) at Mach 1.7 using 100% Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF). The mission sought to balance speed, efficiency, environmental compliance, and comfort, all within the ICAO Chapter 14 noise standards and a maximum takeoff gross weight (TOGW) of approximately 100,000 lb (≈ 45,359 kg)

Objectives

  • Develop a supersonic business jet design suitable for long-haul transoceanic routes.
  • Ensure compliance with environmental standards through reduced noise and full SAF operation.
  • Conduct mission-level and aerodynamic trade studies to optimize lift-to-drag ratio (L/D), thrust, and stability margins.
  • Integrate findings from aerodynamics, propulsion, structure, weights, and performance teams into a unified design solution space.

Methodology

The team implemented a multidisciplinary conceptual design process consisting of:

  • Mission & Technology Trade Matrices to identify constraints and feasible design regions.
  • Parametric Sizing via Hypersonic Convergence (HC) equations to iterate on weight, planform area, and Mach range (1.7–2.5).
  • Aerodynamic database development through literature review of legacy supersonic aircraft (Concorde, Tu-144, Tu-444, Aerion AS2).
  • Propulsion modeling based on the F100-PW-229 turbofan, calibrated for supersonic cruise performance.
  • Configuration Layout integrating internal fuel/structure volumes and external aerodynamic shaping.

The iterative design converged on a configuration featuring high aerodynamic efficiency (L/D ≈ 7–8), a Mach 2.1 design point, and feasible performance across all mission phases.

My Role

As the Aerodynamics & Aerothermodynamics Lead, and a secondary in Stability & Control, I was responsible for:

  • Developing aerodynamic parameters (e.g., lift, drag, pitching moment coefficients) essential for the Hypersonic Convergence synthesis.
  • Performed S&C analyses to determine static margin, control effectiveness, and neutral point location.
  • Deriving realistic aerodynamic performance metrics for the SSBJ’s cruise and climb phases.
  • Delivering verified L/D = 7–8 values to the Synthesis and Trajectory teams for mission performance analysis.
  • Collaborating with stability & control, propulsion, and configuration teams to ensure consistency in external geometry and flow integration.

Outcomes

The SkyBreaker study produced a validated conceptual design that demonstrated both technical and environmental feasibility for future supersonic business travel. The integration of your aerodynamic modeling established a robust foundation for high-speed flight efficiency, shaping the project’s final configuration and performance envelope.

Reverse Engineering SpaceX Martian Starship — Project Details

Overview

This project aimed to reverse-engineer and analyze SpaceX’s Starship for Martian and Earth re-entry conditions. The study focused on achieving high aerodynamic efficiency, controllability, and thermal protection performance during hypersonic descent through rarefied Martian atmosphere and dense Earth reentry flow. The redesigned configuration achieved an estimated lift-to-drag ratio (L/D) of 4–5 on Mars and >8 during Earth reentry, ensuring a smooth, stable descent profile.

Objectives

  • Model Starship geometry and reentry dynamics for both Earth and Mars conditions.
  • Perform aerodynamic and stability analyses to validate lift, drag, and pitching moment coefficients.
  • Evaluate static margin and control ratio for stability across Mach regimes.
  • Propose heat-shield, fin, and strake modifications to enhance control and thermal safety.

Methodology

The team used OpenVSP for 3D modeling, MATLAB for creating an analytical model for aerodynamic coefficient prediction, and for flight performance and control margin calculations. Dynamic stability was analyzed through pitching moment derivatives and control surface sizing, while thermal considerations were informed by convective heating correlations. Analyses compared flight behavior in CO₂-rich Martian atmosphere (low Reynolds number, low density) with Earth’s high dynamic pressure regime, identifying fin and strake adjustments necessary to maintain longitudinal control.

Results & Findings

  • Achieved L/D ≈ 4-5 for Mars entry; >8 for Earth reentry.
  • Stability margin ~10-12% static margin, ensuring passive stability.
  • control ratio >1.5, verifying adequate aerodynamic authority across reentry phases.
  • Strake and fin geometry adjustments reduced pitch oscillations and improved payload stability.
  • Proposed a layered heat shield system with improved fin thermal isolation for reentry resilience.

My Role

As lead for Aero & S&C, I coordinated geometry modifications, aerodynamic analysis, control-surface sizing, and final presentation. Collaborated with RedShift Dynamics team members responsible for structures, thermal systems, and systems integration.

Outcomes

The redesign demonstrated that a refined Starship configuration could achieve the desired Martian glide ratio and control stability, supporting future interplanetary reentry vehicle studies. Recommendations included further CFD validation, material testing, and integration of reaction control system effects for precise descent control.

Formula SAE Aerodynamics Analysis — Project Details

Overview

The A-MOD project focused on redesigning the aerodynamic package of UTA Racing’s A-Modified competition car. Using CFD-based trade studies in STAR-CCM+, the team evaluated how ride height, wing angle, and aero-component interactions affected downforce, drag, and overall balance. The goal was to improve cornering grip and stability without sacrificing straight-line efficiency.

Objectives

  • Increase total downforce while minimizing associated drag penalties.
  • Shift the center of pressure rearward for improved high-speed stability.
  • Develop a validated workflow for repeatable aerodynamic tuning.
  • Provide actionable design recommendations for the vehicle’s competition setup.

Methodology

  • Modeled the full vehicle geometry and simplified underbody for CFD efficiency.
  • Ran 3000+ RANS simulations across parametric sweeps of: ride height, front and rear wing angles of attack, and diffuser performance interactions.
  • Evaluated lift coefficient, drag coefficient, center-of-pressure location, balance percentage, and stability trends and compared the configurations to baseline car to quantify improvements.

Results

  • Angle of attack of around 6° for the rear wing provided favorable balance between downforce and drag. This configuration significantly increases rear load percentage, maintaining control and maximizing performance on track.
  • Front wing's angle of attack can decrease drag but must be carefully managed to avoid shifting the center of pressure too far rearward which can impact the car's handling balance negatively.
  • The best combined performance was found with front wing at 0° angle of attack and the rear wing at 6°. This configuration increased downforce by about 12% and reduced drag by 7%.
  • These gains translate to better cornering grip, more predictable handling, and improved lap-time potential.

My Role

  • Built and cleaned CAD assemblies of the vehicle in SolidWorks.
  • Set up meshing, physics models, and boundary conditions in STAR-CCM+.
  • Designed and executed parametric CFD sweeps.
  • Analyzed trends and presented recommendations to the race car team at UTA.
  • Documented procedure so future teams can iterate on the aero package.

Key Takeaways

  • Learned how CFD trade studies influence real racing performance.
  • Developed comfort tuning aerodynamic systems using engineering judgment.
  • Strengthened skills in STAR-CCM+, SolidWorks, meshing strategy, and aero data interpretation.

PID-Controlled Ball Balancing Platform — Project Details

Overview

Developed a two-axis ball-balancing platform capable of maintaining a steel ball's position on a resistive touchscreen using a closed-loop PID control algorithm. The system utilized a Raspberry Pi Pico microcontroller for real-time processing and two servo motors to control the platform’s tilt. The project demonstrated principles of real-time feedback, signal acquisition, and precision mechatronic actuation.

Methodology

  • Implemented a PID controller in C to compute proportional, integral, and derivative corrections at a 50 Hz control rate.
  • Acquired X–Y position feedback via the TSC2007 I2C touchscreen controller, processed analog voltage data into calibrated pixel coordinates.
  • Generated PWM signals to command servo angles, compensating for positional error through dynamic tilt adjustments.
  • Integrated UART and Bluetooth modules for wireless telemetry, enabling real-time visualization and remote gain tuning.
  • Applied digital filtering to minimize sensor noise and ensure loop stability under transient disturbances.

Results

  • Achieved ~5% steady-state positioning accuracy under dynamic input conditions.
  • Maintained stable response with settling time under 1.2 s for 2 cm step changes.
  • Demonstrated effective PID gain optimization (Kp = 0.45, Ki = 0.07, Kd = 0.12) balancing response speed and overshoot.
  • Verified robustness through repeatability tests and Bluetooth-based parameter adjustments.

Key Takeaways

  • Gained hands-on experience in embedded control systems, including I2C, UART, and PWM protocol integration.
  • Learned PID tuning techniques for nonlinear systems with coupled axes and mechanical inertia.
  • Strengthened understanding of real-time systems programming, noise mitigation, and actuator dynamics.
  • Reinforced principles of system modeling, feedback control, and mechatronic system integration-essential foundations for robotics and flight control design.

Modal Analysis of a Piston Rod — Project Details

Overview

This project investigated the dynamic behavior of a forged 4340 steel piston rodfrom a Toyota 1.8L engine using both experimental modal testing and finite element modal analysis. The objective was to extract the rod’s natural frequencies and mode shapes, then validate the simulation against measured resonance peaks to quantify modeling accuracy.

Objectives

  • Identify key natural frequencies governing dynamic response under excitation.
  • Visualize and interpret mode shapes to understand likely deformation mechanisms.
  • Build an ANSYS modal model and validate with experimental FFT data using percent error.
  • Link results to real engineering impact: vibration mitigation, fatigue prevention, and mechanical reliability.

Methodology

  • Experimental setup: suspended the piston rod using a thin string to approximate free-free boundary conditions.
  • Applied impulse excitation using a hammer strike and recorded dynamic response.
  • Performed frequency analysis using an audio spectrum analyzer (FFT) to identify resonance peaks.
  • FEA workflow: created/cleaned rod geometry in SolidWorks, then ran ANSYS modal analysis to extract mode shapes and natural frequencies.
  • Validated results by comparing simulation frequencies to measured resonance peaks and computing error percentage.

Results

  • Extracted multiple vibration modes (Mode 1 through Mode 6) using Ansys Mechanical modal analysis.
  • Experimentally identified corresponding resonance peaks and mapped them to simulated modal frequencies.
  • Achieved a validated simulation pipeline by correlating mode shapes + resonance behavior across test and FEA.

My Role

  • Built the Ansys modal analysis model and executed structural dynamics simulations.
  • Supported experimental modal testing workflow (free-free suspension + impulse excitation).
  • Interpreted FFT response and performed simulation-to-experiment correlation.
  • Created mode-shape visualizations and technical documentation for final reporting.

Key Takeaways

  • Learned how suspension assumptions (free-free vs constrained) significantly influence extracted modal frequencies.
  • Strengthened ability to correlate real measurement signals to simulation outputs (FFT peak identification → mode mapping).
  • Developed practical judgment in structural dynamics + validation — essential for aerospace structures, EV components, robotics frames, and thermal/mechanical packaging.
  • Improved fluency in ANSYS modal workflows, mode interpretation, and engineering communication of dynamic risk.
